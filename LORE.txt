Shatterlands: World Lore

The Unbroken Age
----------------
Before the Shatterlands there was only the Floor: one seamless plain of dirt
and stone, resting on Bedrock that nothing could break. The material was all
there, but nobody could separate it into pieces small enough to use.

The First Blast
---------------
A surveyor set a charge beneath the plain. The blast is remembered, but her
name was lost long ago. The ground came apart into thousands of tiny cubes.
Those who watched swore that what the blast took was what fell. Miners call
this the Scoop Law. It is their ideal rather than a promise. Dust drifts
away, and the sharpest-eyed miners say a little is always missing.

The Hanging Ground
------------------
Miners learned to spot shelves of earth held up by little more than a narrow
neck of stone. Cut one loose and the whole shelf can turn over as it falls.

The Bedrock Line
----------------
The blasts could never touch the bottom layer, and no tool has ever
scratched it. The miners treat it as the one promise the land keeps: you can
ruin everything above it, but never fall through.

The Miners
----------
A miner carries three tools and a field checklist. The miner you play is a
man who makes his living breaking ground and delivering stone.

Tools of the Trade
------------------
The Impact Hammer takes a measured bite with every swing. It’s for shaping a
crater, trimming a ledge, or finding where a structure is weakest.

Miners throw a Breaching Charge in an arc, wait for it to land, then set it
off by hand. They use one charge at a time, and every charge packs the same
punch.

The Blower draws loose rubble into its nozzle. Only stone goes into the
miner’s tally. At high power it reaches farther and pulls harder.

Reading the Ground
------------------
A blast is felt as a shake in the world itself. Fire, smoke and dust rise
and fade, but the cracks stay. Experienced miners read those cracks first.
An unmarked ledge may already be hanging by a thread. Deep cracks mean time
is running out.

The oldest rule of the miners’ guidebook is to look at what’s holding a
thing up before you strike it. Anyone can make a hole, but a miner makes the
ground drop where they want it to.

Stone Deliveries
----------------
After a blast, miners gather what stone they can and log it in the Storage
tab of their field checklist. When they have enough stored, they turn it in
for a delivery. Every delivery pays the same. Five hundred stone, one
hundred credits. Old miners can tell you how to bring down a ledge with one
charge. Ask them where the stone goes and they’ll tell you how much you’re
short.

The Echoes
----------
Miners can photograph a real object from the world outside, such as a shed,
a chair or a boot, and carry it to a scanning post. The post builds it from
cubes in the material the miner names, wood, stone, concrete or earth, and
stands it in the Shatterlands. This is called an Echo. It’s never the object
itself, only what the post remembers of it, and it can be cracked, cut and
blasted like anything else.

Echoes are never lost for good, because the post keeps every scan. Many
miners return to the same one again and again, to see how it falls from a
different angle.

The miners say the Shatterlands aren’t a ruin.
They’re a place where every wreck can be rebuilt and wrecked again.

Shatterlands: Player Profile

The Miner
---------
The player is a man who makes his living breaking ground and delivering
stone. He carries an Impact Hammer, breaching charges, a Blower and a field
checklist. His name, age and life before mining are open.

He can spend most of a shift vacuuming rubble and still take pride in the
few seconds it took to bring a ledge down exactly where he wanted it. He
judges a blast by where the stone lands. If it falls within reach, he's
saved himself a long afternoon.

The First Camp
--------------
While looking for a place to work, the miner comes across a patch of land
with an old survey post marking the First Blast. He stops to read it.

    FIRST BLAST SURVEY
    Bedrock untouched.
    Loose stone recovered.
    Second count: short.

There's a mountain nearby. He sets off a charge against its side and opens a
hollow big enough to shelter in. When the dust clears, he checks the cracks
above the entrance, brings his gear inside and lays out his bedroll. The
stone from the blast is still lying outside. In the morning, it'll go toward
his first delivery.

The Work
--------
A shift is mostly familiar work. Read the ground, break it, draw the loose
rubble into the Blower, and put the recovered stone into storage through the
field checklist. A delivery takes five hundred stored stone and pays one
hundred credits.

The miner gets better at choosing where to strike. Some days he chips away
at a bank. Other days he finds the narrow neck holding up a shelf of earth
and brings it down with one charge. Either way, he still has to gather the
stone. On a bad afternoon he puts his hammer away, checks the tally, and
takes it back out for the last twelve cubes.

The Questions He Carries
------------------------
The surveyor's name is lost. The post says the second count came up short,
but it leaves the miner to wonder what was missing. Where the delivered
stone goes is another question. Ask an old miner and they'll tell you how
much you're short.

Game Status
-----------
The miner, his tools and the delivery terms come from the existing lore and
beta. The survey post, its inscription and the mountain camp are proposed
opening story beats. The post and camp are not implemented features of the
current beta.
