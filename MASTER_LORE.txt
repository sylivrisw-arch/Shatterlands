SHATTERLANDS
WORLD LORE

THE CUTTER
==========
Your work is to open the ridges of the Shatterlands and uncover the ground beneath them.
There is no prescribed shape to leave behind.
Choose a face, read what holds it, and decide where to strike.
You carry three tools.
The Impact Hammer asks what a small cut will reveal.
TNT asks what a whole face will do when it is shaken.
The Blower asks what has been hiding beneath the spoil.
You move on foot across the terraces and along the feet of the ridges.
When a slope still looks like one solid mass, lift into the air and take a higher view, then come back down to the edge you mean to open.

THE LAND
========
The ground climbs in stages.
Broad terraces step up into rolling hills.
Rivers wander the lower ground.
From a distance, a slope can look like one solid mass.
Up close, every edge offers somewhere to begin.
A cut changes more than the face it leaves behind.
It exposes the ground above it, opens a hollow beneath a shelf, or leaves a thin connection carrying what once rested on a hillside.

THE TERRACES
============
Do not mistake steady for safe.
A cut beneath a shelf may remove the last thing holding it up.

THE HILLS AND THE MOUNTAINS
===========================
Blasted cubes are kicked downhill and roll off mountain faces.
The mountains are the great faces.
From the foot of one, it looks like a single mass with no seam in it.

HANGING GROUND
==============
Look at what holds a ledge before looking at where it might fall.
Some cuts give way at once.
Others leave a shelf hanging while the dust settles.
A second strike can change the balance.
A blast can leave damage around the hollow it opened.
The ground may answer after the charge has gone quiet.
An overhang that remains standing deserves another look.
Its shape alone does not tell you how the next cut will behave.
Trust no shelf because it stood after one strike.
A ledge that stands is not a ledge that will stand.
Work a face, step back, and study what has changed.

THE BEDROCK
===========
Beneath the worked earth lies Bedrock.
The Hammer can expose it and a charge can clear the ground above it.
Neither opens a way through.
Whatever shape the excavation takes, that floor remains.
Each time you dig, it is put back where it was.
The boundary you find is the one you will find again.

THE SPOIL
=========
A blast leaves its own landscape: cubes across the floor, piles against the slope, loose pieces finding their way down into the hollow.
Watch where they land.
A successful cut can leave a face buried beneath its own rubble.
Sometimes the next useful action is to clear the view and inspect the ground that remains.
Loose cubes can roll free on a slope you thought was settled.

THE IMPACT HAMMER
=================
The Hammer asks what a small cut will reveal.
It makes a close, deliberate mark: an edge trimmed, a hollow opened, a little ground taken from beneath a larger formation.
The mark is small enough to examine before you choose the next one.
A small cut is not always a quiet one.
The ground around it can be weakened.
A shelf may give way at once or hang for a while before it answers.
Strike, step back, and watch what the ground does next.

TNT
===
TNT asks what a whole face will do when it is shaken.
You begin with a charge already in hand.
Only one charge is yours at a time.
Once thrown, it remains yours until it goes off or is lost.
Then the next one is ready.
Draw back and throw.
Watch where it lands, then choose when it goes off.
A throw that hits nothing is lost, so aim with the shape of the ridge in mind.
The blast opens a hollow in a single stroke.
That is only part of what it does.
Fire rolls out from the cut.
Loose pieces are flung across the slope.
The force of the blast shoves rubble already lying nearby.
The ground around the hollow is left weakened.
A shelf above the cut may begin to fall, or hang until long after the charge has gone quiet.

THE BLOWER
==========
The Blower asks what has been hiding beneath the spoil.
Hold it toward the rubble and the loose cubes are drawn to the nozzle.
What it takes is gone.
The spoil is not carried with you or set down somewhere else.
It runs at two strengths.
At the higher one it pulls harder and reaches farther.
Use it when the spoil lies far off.
Clear the view, then look at the exposed ground that remains.

THE WORK
========
Walk the base of a ridge or take a higher view.
Find a face worth opening.
Choose a small strike or a charge.
Watch the first movement, then the ground above it.
Clear the spoil and return to the exposed edge.
The Shatterlands reveal their shape through the work done upon them: the hollow opened, the ledge brought down, and the bedrock left bare.

THE GROUND
==========
You are the one who reads the ground.
The Shatterlands do not explain themselves.
They show their shape only to the one who keeps cutting, stepping back, and looking again.
