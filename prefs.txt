SHATTERLANDS BETA MASTER REFERENCE
==================================

[VERIFIED BY SOURCE REVIEW, 2026-11-09]
Current sections below are based on Shatterlands-beta(1).py (14,033 lines).
The launcher, embedded engine and embedded game passed syntax compilation.
Gameplay and Windows launch were not tested.

1. CURRENT BUILD
================

Launcher          | Shatterlands - beta, 380 × 500 nominal, single Launch game button.
Settings          | Tab → Display, Scans, Controls. Display has Resolution, Frame Rate and Debris sections.
Display defaults  | Fullscreen, 1920 × 1080 canvas, 120% scene scale, 60 FPS limit, VSync off.
Cube detail       | 2 cubes per block edge. Set cubedetail-N (1–50) or cubedetail-max at launch.
Cache             | cache beside the script by default. SHATTERLANDS_CACHE_DIR overrides the path.
Launcher config   | engineui_config.json keeps launch commands. Checklist progress is session-only.
Debris            | On, 100% amount. Optional 12,000 loose-debris cap starts off.
Map               | 15 × 15 chunks, 15 blocks per chunk, 225 blocks across.
Tool slots        | 1 Impact Hammer, 2 TNT, 3 Blower, 4 Checklist.
Hammer            | Left-click to swing at fixed 1×.
TNT               | Hold/release left-click to throw. Left-click after landing to detonate. One charge at fixed 1.5×.
Blower            | Hold left-click for suction. Scroll down/up for 1×/2×. Stores only stone and clears other swallowed debris.
Checklist         | Q or slot 4 + left-click. View 500-stone stacks and edit six personal task rows.
Storage           | Checklist tab. Deposit all carried stone or withdraw up to 500.
Jobs              | Checklist tab. Deliver 500 stored stone for 100 credits. Separate from the six personal tasks.
Upgrades          | Checklist tab. Cost = 100 × (current level + 1). Purchase increments the level counter.
Tools             | Checklist tab with tool help.
World storage     | The hut, physical storage crates and B-key storage are absent.
Target display    | The old cube-selection outline is absent. TNT can still show a charge label when aimed at.
Reset damage      | Clears carried stone, active charges, debris and structures. Stored stone and credits stay in memory.

Progress starts fresh each game launch. ChecklistProgress initializes stored stone,
credits, completed jobs and upgrade level to zero. InventoryPanel creates six empty
personal task rows. There is no HQ progress file read or write in this build.

Current scan importer
---------------------
Put .cells files beside the game script, in the working directory, in scans/
beside the script, or in ~/Downloads/scan_exports. Tab → Scans lists the exports
and polls while open. Scans are resampled to the current cube detail and placed
about four blocks ahead as fixed structures. The parser reads materials and
[x, y, half_thickness, material_index] rows in cells. cubes_per_block defaults to 5.
The format tag is not validated. Dirt, stone, wood and white concrete have
material mappings. Leaf becomes planks. The external scanner remains unverified.

2. [EARLIER SOURCE REVIEW] IMPLEMENTED BEHAVIOR AND DEPARTURES FROM DESIGN INTENT
=================================================================================

Behavior recorded in the earlier source
---------------------------------------
- Cube detail is now fixed per run and defaults to 2 cubes per block (a cube is 0.5 block). CUBE_DETAIL_DEFAULT = 2 (G236). There is no cube-detail slider in the menu any more: you set it with the launch command cubedetail-N (N = 1 to 50) or cubedetail-max, in the launcher's cog dialog. The old docs said 5; that is no longer the default. The game resamples scans to the current detail and uses 5 as a fallback when a scan omits cubes_per_block; the scanner exporter itself was not verified.
- Scanned objects (.cells files) are placed as ordinary fixed Structures, resampled to the current cube detail, so they can be cut, cracked and blasted.
- The game sets struct_res equal to the cube detail (G3055, and every frame at G3373), so ordinary structure cells use the ground's cell size. Ground debris is half-sized, and large ground slabs may be coarsened; matching size also does not guarantee lattice-aligned placement. The engine's own default (STRUCT_RES = 2, E1899) is only used if the game does not override it.
- Blast debris cubes and blast effect cubes are sized in ground cubes, so they follow the cube detail.
- STRUCT_DEBRIS_SPLIT = 1 (E1901): a destroyed structure cell is offered as one cube of its own size before debris settings and collision filters (2 requests eight half-size cubes per cell).
- SCOOP_STRICT = True (G1620): initial game-side blast sampling budgets are bypassed; one debris candidate per removed ground cube is requested. This is not guaranteed conservation. The spawn calls do not pass keep_all=True, so debris amount/on-off settings and terrain/structure collision filtering still discard candidates. C4_EXACT_MAX and C4_MAX_CUBES do not limit the initial sample while it is on (C4_EXACT_MAX still sets an awake-budget floor) (see the departures below). Set it to False to bring back the old budgets.
- Small loose pieces crumble: a broken-off piece of 1.5 cubic blocks or less (CRUMBLE_BODY_VOLUME, G66) is turned into loose debris cubes by crumble_small_bodies (G69, called each frame at G3483) instead of falling as one big rigid cube.

Where the game still departs from the rules
-------------------------------------------
Rule 1 (lattice cubes):
- Matching structure cell size does not guarantee alignment with the terrain lattice. _stand_structure uses the supplied origin without snapping; scans are positioned relative to the player.
- Blast effect cubes (fire, smoke, dust, sparks) are free-floating visual cubes of varying size, not on the lattice.
- Ground slabs that fall as one rigid body use coarser cells (2x2x2 up to 6x6x6) when they hold more than 5000 cells (max_cells in add_ground_body, E7550).
- The held tools (hammer, cannon, TNT, Blower), the planted TNT box (0.42 block) and the cannonball (1.5 ground cubes across, so not a whole number of cells) are drawn from boxes, not on the lattice.
- The Blower's spray cubes are full ground-cube size but are fired as a moving stream, not placed on the lattice.

Rule 2 (scoop rule):
- Debris cube size does not match. Ground debris from TNT, hammer and cannon is drawn at C4_DEBRIS_SIZE (0.5) of a ground cube's edge. The ground-clearance factor C4_DEBRIS_SCALE is now 1.0 (it was 0.82), so the size is 0.5, not about 0.41. Neither count conservation nor matching size is guaranteed. (DirtCubes.spawn, E2788, sets the edge as size_scale * cell / level.)
- Cut-off ground that is too small to become a slab (under SLAB_MIN_CELLS, 1500) crumbles to cubes. The old cap of SLAB_DEBRIS_MAX (3000 per blast) is skipped while SCOOP_STRICT is on, but the later spawn filters and debris settings can still drop cubes.
- The earlier source used a 12,000-cube loose-debris limit at full debris amount. The beta cap is optional and starts off. Spawn filtering and awake-cube budgets are separate from the loose-debris cap.

Related engine facts from the earlier source
--------------------------------------------
- STRUCT_UNBREAKABLE_CELLS is 40000 (was 2000), so shapes at full cube detail can still collapse.
- Beta TNT is fixed at 1.5×. Section 4.2 retains the earlier 1× values as a tuning reference.
- The map is smaller: MAP_RADIUS = 7 (G1454), 7 chunks each way from spawn (15 x 15 chunks, 225 blocks across), loaded once. It used to be 15.
- The test cube (BENCH_*) is gone from the engine and the game.

7. [DESIGN INTENT / FICTION] RULES AND LORE
===========================================

[DESIGN INTENT — NOT AN IMPLEMENTATION GUARANTEE] Cube and scoop rules
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Everything in the world is built from the same small cubes as the terrain (one cell = 1/cube-detail block). Structures use the engine's own cell grid at struct_res, which follows cube detail, and their cell edges line up with the ground cubes' edges. No separate coarser cell size for test shapes or new objects.
2. Scoop rule: what a blast removes is what comes out as debris. A destroyed structure cell becomes exactly one cube of its own size; a ground cube dug out becomes one debris cube. No splitting a cell into several, no inventing extra pieces.
3. New things are made the same way from here on: terrain cubes and structure cubes, differing only in material.

Lore
----

The Shatterlands

The Unbroken Age. Before the Shatterlands there was only the Floor: one seamless plain of dirt and stone, resting on Bedrock that nothing could break. The material was all there, but nobody could separate it into pieces small enough to use.

The First Blast. A surveyor set a charge beneath the plain. The blast is remembered, but her name was lost long ago. The ground came apart into thousands of tiny cubes, and they were thrown into the air and fell again. Those who watched swore that what the blast took was what fell, and the Breakers still hold to this as the Scoop Law. It is their ideal rather than a promise. Dust drifts away, and the sharpest-eyed Breakers say a little is always missing.

The Hanging Ground. A severed ledge can fall as one great slab, turning as it drops. Survivors learned to read the land for these shelves of earth, held up by little more than a narrow neck of stone.

The Bedrock Line. The blasts could never touch the bottom layer, and no tool has ever scratched it. The survivors treat it as the one promise the land keeps: you can ruin everything above it, but never fall through.

The Breakers. A Breaker carries three tools and a field checklist.

The Impact Hammer takes a measured bite with every swing. It’s for shaping a crater, trimming a ledge, or finding where a structure is weakest.
Breaching Charges are thrown in an arc and set off by hand. Every charge packs the same punch, and Breakers trust them to bring down large structures.
The Blower draws in loose rubble and keeps the stone. At high power it reaches farther and pulls harder.

A blast is felt as a shake in the world itself. Fire, smoke and dust rise and fade, but the cracks stay. Experienced Breakers read those cracks first. An unmarked ledge may already be hanging by a thread. Deep cracks mean time is running out.

The oldest rule of the Breakers is to look at what’s holding a thing up before you strike it. Anyone can make a hole, but a Breaker makes the ground drop where they want it to.

The Stone Ledger. After a blast, Breakers tally loose stone in stacks of five hundred. They track their own tasks in the field checklist and can put stone into storage. The quartermaster pays one hundred credits for a delivery of five hundred stored stone.

The Echoes. Breakers can photograph a real object from the world outside, such as a shed, a chair or a boot, and carry it to a scanning post. The post builds it from cubes in the material the Breaker names, wood, stone, concrete or earth, and stands it in the Shatterlands. This is called an Echo. It’s never the object itself, only what the post remembers of it, and it can be cracked, cut and blasted like anything else.

Echoes are never lost for good, because the post keeps every scan. Many Breakers return to the same one again and again, to see how it falls from a different angle.

The Breakers say the Shatterlands aren’t a ruin. They’re a place where every wreck can be rebuilt and wrecked again.
